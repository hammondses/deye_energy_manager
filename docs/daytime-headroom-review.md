# Daytime charge headroom review

This is a read-only policy review, not a runtime change. The immediate concern was whether the battery command can consume headroom too early and thereby lose otherwise-capturable PV above the AC/export path.

The present battery action in `solar_advisory.py` is the greater of (a) the least DC source power needed to meet the next completion-trajectory floor and (b) current DC PV that would otherwise exceed house-plus-export AC capability. Both are capped by live PV, BMS acceptance and the configured DC limit. This is already a useful “completion floor or current spill” rule. The physical clear-sky replay is diagnostic; it does not currently impose a future headroom ceiling. It also intentionally assumes no future EV load.

I did not find a same-input deterministic example where reducing that action preserved the same completion constraints and captured more clipping. Reducing below the completion floor can break the conservative forecast trajectory; reducing current spill can only defer the same capture when later clipping remains available. The offline `tools/replay_daytime_headroom.py` reproduces one tapered-battery case: 32 kWh capacity, 50% initial SOC, 100% target, 13.5 kW BMS/DC cap, taper above 85%, current/physical PV 22 kW DC, 4 kW house load, and a flat 12 kW conservative PV proxy. The current command captures 17.021 kWh of the 76 kWh physical clipping potential and reaches 32 kWh. Requesting 0, 1 or 3 kW in the first five-minute interval also reaches 32 kWh and captures the same 17.021 kWh because later clipped intervals still have enough acceptance to fill the remaining capacity. This is not evidence of an early-fill loss; it shows why an invented headroom cap would add complexity without improving that case.

The real tension is forecast uncertainty. The conservative completion trajectory uses Solcast's AC-output values unchanged as a DC-available proxy, while the separate physical replay can contain much more DC power than the export/inverter path. If the proxy is low and physical DC later is high, charging now to protect the lower completion trajectory can reduce later clipping headroom. Relaxing that floor may capture more under the bright physical realization, but it no longer guarantees completion under the conservative forecast realization. Such a result must be shown as a scenario tradeoff, not as an avoidable loss or a guarantee that both goals are met.

For a future optimizer, keep the two limits explicit:

* `E_floor(t)`: the minimum stored energy now that can still reach the daily target by the solar deadline under the selected conservative forecast, household load and SOC-dependent acceptance curve.
* `R_clip(t)`: the maximum additional stored energy the battery can accept from future physical clipping, integrating each interval's spill, BMS limit, SOC-dependent charge limit, efficiency and remaining capacity.
* A diagnostic headroom ceiling can be reported as `E_target - R_clip(t)` (bounded at zero and target), but only as a physical-scenario quantity. It must not override `E_floor` silently. If `E_floor > E_target - R_clip`, report a completion-versus-capture conflict and the assumptions behind it.

The interval spill term is `max(P_dc_physical - min(P_ac_limit, P_nonEV_house_ac + export_limit) / inverter_efficiency, 0)`. The accepted charge in each interval is additionally bounded by the live/configured DC maximum, BMS limit, the charge-acceptance curve at that SOC, and battery headroom. Because the completion objective and clipping opportunity can share the same PV, summing them independently would double-count energy; the model should evaluate both in a single chronological trajectory or compare clearly labelled scenarios.

Two gaps remain relevant to interpreting the projection: future EV charging is omitted from the physical capture replay, and the completion forecast uses an AC-output proxy rather than an independently validated DC forecast. The former can overstate projected clipping while an EV is expected to remain plugged in; the latter can make completion floors conservative when substantial DC clipping is physically available. These do not justify a fixed SOC ceiling. A useful next advisory is to publish the separate completion floor, scenario headroom reserve, and conflict flag, while retaining the existing floor/current-spill command until replay against site history shows a repeatable, avoidable loss.

The synthetic replay is not a seasonal or forecast-calibration validation and does not establish that the current policy is globally optimal. It only fails to support adding a headroom cap on the basis of this counterexample search.

The 22 kW synthetic stress case deliberately exceeds this installation’s 18 kW configured PV ceiling; it is an algorithm sensitivity example, not a site forecast. Reproduce it with `python3 tools/replay_daytime_headroom.py`.
